Where Did the Rice Purity Test Come From? Tracing the Quiz Back to Rice University

Millions of people have taken the Rice Purity Test, posted their scores, and debated what the numbers mean — but far fewer know the story behind it. So where did the rice purity test come from? The answer takes us to Houston, Texas, and the campus of Rice University, where the quiz began life long before the internet existed as a playful tradition among students.

The earliest versions of the test were simple paper checklists passed around dorm rooms. New students would tick off statements describing things they had done — from innocent adventures like sneaking out after curfew to wilder college experiences — and then compare their totals with friends. The word “purity” was always tongue-in-cheek: a perfect score of 100 did not mean someone was virtuous, only that they had answered “no” to every item on the list. It was a bonding ritual, a way for freshmen to laugh together during orientation and discover how sheltered — or adventurous — their new classmates had been.

For decades the test stayed a campus affair, photocopied and handed down from one class to the next. That changed when students began posting the question list on early internet forums and personal websites. Freed from the campus, the quiz found a much bigger audience: high schoolers curious about college life, friend groups looking for a party game, and eventually entire generations discovering it through social media. Each new platform gave it fresh momentum, from chain emails to Facebook notes to TikTok score reveals.

The questions themselves have evolved along the way. The classic list contains one hundred items, but many online versions tweak the wording, reorder the questions, or add modern references so the quiz feels current. The core idea never changed, though: one hundred yes-or-no questions, one point subtracted for every “yes,” and a final score between 0 and 100 that sparks conversation.

Why did this particular quiz survive when countless other chain questionnaires faded away? Part of the answer is the scoring system. A single number is endlessly shareable — it fits in a tweet, a caption, or a group chat — and it invites comparison without revealing any specifics about what you actually checked off. The test also arrived at the perfect cultural moment again and again: each wave of new teenagers rediscovers it as if it were brand new, keeping the tradition alive.

Today the Rice Purity Test is a genuine piece of internet folklore. It has been translated into other languages, parodied, analyzed by campus newspapers, and taken by celebrities. Yet its heart is still that dorm-room checklist: a lighthearted way to reflect on your experiences and bond with friends over shared stories.
