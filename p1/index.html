Is the Rice Purity Test Safe? What You Should Know Before Taking the Quiz

The Rice Purity Test has taken the internet by storm. What started as a lighthearted survey among Rice University students decades ago is now a viral sensation, shared by millions of teenagers and college students across TikTok, Instagram, and group chats. With its growing popularity, one question keeps coming up again and again: is the Rice Purity Test safe to take? If you are thinking about answering the 100 questions yourself, or you are a parent wondering whether your teen should, this guide walks you through everything you need to know about the safety, privacy, and content of the test.

First, it helps to understand what the Rice Purity Test actually is. The test is a simple checklist of one hundred yes-or-no questions about life experiences — things like holding hands, going on dates, breaking curfews, drinking, and other coming-of-age moments. You tick every statement that applies to you, and the quiz calculates a score between 0 and 100. A score of 100 means you answered “no” to everything, while a lower score means you have checked off more experiences. There is no pass or fail, and the number is meant to be a fun conversation starter rather than a serious measurement of character.

From a technical standpoint, the quiz itself is harmless. Reputable versions of the test run entirely in your web browser. You do not need to create an account, hand over your email address, or install anything. Your answers are processed on your own device and are never sent to a server, which means there is no database of your responses sitting somewhere on the internet. That design is a big part of why so many people feel comfortable taking it.

Privacy is where you should still exercise a little caution. Because the test is so popular, dozens of copycat websites host their own versions, and not all of them are equally trustworthy. Before you start clicking, take a quick look at the site you are using. A safe version will never ask for your name, phone number, email, or social media login. If a page demands personal details before showing your score, close the tab and find another version. Sticking to well-known, established quiz pages is the simplest way to protect yourself.

Content-wise, the questions are suggestive but not explicit. They reference dating, parties, alcohol, and mild rule-breaking — topics most high school and college students already encounter in everyday conversation. Nothing in the standard 100 questions is graphic, and nothing asks you to do anything illegal or dangerous. That said, the quiz was originally written for college students, so younger teens may find a few questions surprising. Parents who are unsure can simply read through the question list first; it only takes a couple of minutes.

There is also an emotional side to consider. Scores have a way of spreading through friend groups, and it is easy to feel judged when everyone is comparing numbers. Remember that the score is not a grade on your worth as a person. Two people with the same life experiences can interpret questions differently, and cultural or religious values shape answers in ways a number can never capture. Treat the result as entertainment, share it only if you want to, and never let anyone pressure you into taking the test or revealing your score.

So, is the rice purity test safe? For most people, on a reputable site, the answer is yes. The quiz collects no personal data, asks nothing dangerous, and takes only a few minutes. Keep your personal information to yourself, use a trusted version of the test, and enjoy it for what it is — a playful snapshot of your experiences so far.
